# Kite landing page design

The landing page at `/` and `/landing` presents tokenized stock research, basket purchases and vaultless recurring investing with concrete product language. Mainnet spot purchases, the devnet wallet recurring demonstration and paper practice remain explicitly separated.

## Direction and references

- [Kimia](https://www.kimia.live/): architectural imagery, concise product language, editorial spacing and disciplined section grids.
- [Griffin](https://www.griffin.com/): quiet financial typography, product previews and visual explanations of infrastructure.
- The user's local `kimia.mp4` and `Griffin.mp4` recordings were inspected as design references. Both are 1920 × 1020 at 30fps; their durations are approximately 46.77 and 42.77 seconds. This is an adaptation for Kite, not a frame-for-frame port of either site's branding or animation.
- [21st Financial Platform Feature Grid](https://21st.dev/@uiable/components/block-feature-32), component 29553, informed feature composition. The follow-up 21st scroll-story search informed numbered steps and progress treatment; additional retrieval was blocked by its daily limit. No third-party component code was installed. Existing Kite Brand, AssetAvatar, Change, ThemeToggle and SocialLinks are reused.
- The requested scroll motion uses pinned GSAP 3.15.0 and @gsap/react 2.1.2, following the official [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/), [React cleanup](https://gsap.com/resources/React/) and [matchMedia](<https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/>) patterns. All other lockfile resolutions are preserved.

The page keeps Kite's forest/cream/lime identity. Local Manrope is paired with a Georgia italic display accent. Corners and shadows are restrained; financial values use tabular numbers.

The latest revision uses the user's `DollarAbstract.png` as the hero artwork. Its currency engraving and green geometry are composed beside the headline on desktop and beneath it on phones. `wallstreet.png` is a Wall Street film poster with a tagline and production credits; it informs the editorial direction but is not placed in the landing page. The latest contextual 21st search was blocked by workspace data-egress review, so this revision uses Kite's existing primitives without uploading repository context or installing component code.

## Product and data behavior

- The market snapshot uses observed, verified, non-halted token assets from the existing market snapshot. Its heading and market link sit above four equal quote columns on desktop and a two-column grid on tablet/phones. Company identity, token symbol, USD price and 24-hour change have distinct aligned positions; snapshot dates and partial/stale-data notices remain below the quotes. No placeholder quotes are seeded when data is unavailable.
- The latest layout places this snapshot beneath the research workspace, outside its finite pin, aligned with the chapter's content rather than nested container padding. Company names and token symbols remain distinct visible labels; avatars retain accessible company labels. Its observed-price footer is unchanged.
- Both landing routes are explicitly dynamic. Their shared market service caches observations; route rendering never changes from static to dynamic when a later request reads Redis or live market sources.
- Both routes use the same Next `after` background-refresh contract as the market API. Previously observed data or verified catalog identity can render without waiting for the full price refresh. Missing observations remain missing and dates/refreshing states remain visible; the shared cache and provider rules are unchanged.
- Research loads through the shared research client when its preview approaches the viewport. It uses actual underlying share history, indicators, financial statements, news and events. Token prices and historical share closes are labeled separately. Missing data shows an unavailable state.
- Research requests, refresh timers and observers are cleaned up. A changed mint cannot display the prior mint's research. Cached research remains dated and is labeled when refresh is unavailable.
- Baskets use current SDK definitions and weights. The custom builder's 2–12 asset range is explicitly separate from execution availability.
- The recurring diagram is a labeled illustration: 100 test KUSD per week, 12 weeks and a 1,200 test KUSD funding limit. It does not represent a user's portfolio or confirmed execution.
- Wallet recurring collects test KUSD and mints test stock outputs on devnet. Mainnet recurring remains unavailable. Paper schedules run only while the app is open and do not backfill missed intervals.

## Motion and accessibility

The latest user request supersedes the discrete Griffin-inspired dock: one fixed, centered navigation surface now morphs continuously over the first 180px of scrolling. Its measured width, padding, height, wordmark and themed surface transition together, with 0.2-second scrub smoothing. The geometric home glyph stays usable in the island. No text scaling or header layout shift is used. The desktop theme toggle fades/collapses during the transition, becomes inert when concealed and returns at the top; island width excludes it and uses the same gaps as the rendered layout so Open Kite fits. A focused theme control transfers focus to Open Kite before being concealed. Scrolling upward reverses the morph; reduced motion switches directly. Bounded mobile navigation retains its menu theme controls, Escape/focus restoration and outside-click dismissal.

The motion thesis is the recurring authorization-to-delivery relationship. The focal story explains four states: wallet custody, bounded plan authorization, a due installment and test-stock delivery to owner wallet accounts. A growing connector, due-installment pulse and stock-output reveal follow those states; illustrative funding amounts never count up or imply confirmed activity.

Research advances through Overview, Technicals, Fundamentals, News and Events; recurring advances through its four explanation steps. Each section owns a single normalized, scroll-scrubbed GSAP driver. There is no autoplay or timer fallback on any device. Scrolling backward reverses each story, and stopping holds the current progress.

Animated product chapters use finite GSAP pins. Research and recurring pin the complete copy/workspace pair when it fits with 96px top clearance and 24px below; otherwise a fitting workspace alone is pinned. Basket assembly and ownership likewise fall back to the smaller allocation panel or steps group when the full chapter is too tall. At viewport heights up to 820px, reduced vertical spacing preserves font sizes, disclosures and 44px controls. Research panes and source notices use bounded, keyboard-scrollable areas at all sizes so loading, long news and stale-data notices cannot change the pin's height. Truly oversized stages stay inline. Research/recurring distance is 0.62 viewport heights per transition; allocation/ownership use 0.9 viewport heights. Timelines read ScrollTrigger progress directly on scroll and refresh, rather than depending on a detached proxy tween after responsive reconstruction. All timelines reach their completed frame at 86% of the pin, then hold through the remaining 14% before releasing. Pending reading entrances in the pinned content finish before that hold. Native scroll anchoring is disabled only inside the landing page to prevent viewport jumps during pin-spacer rebuilding. There is no wheel/touch interception, scroll replacement or mandatory snapping. Persistent panes/captions keep stable heights; React updates only at stage boundaries.

Pause freezes the illustration, not page scrolling. Direct selection holds a chapter until the next actual scroll; Follow scroll resynchronizes it with the current position. Research does not automatically hide a keyboard-focused article or link. The active tab scrolls into the horizontal tab strip without moving the page.

The authored entry coordinates a 1.15-second artwork settle with two masked headline lines, supporting copy and the wallet-ownership detail. Image parallax and the entrance affect different elements so their transforms do not compete. At the user's request, below-fold reading text now fades upward on its first viewport approach, informed by locally inspected `kimia.mp4` frames. Headings rise 28px and supporting copy 18px, using a 0.72-second power3 ease-out and a short 0.065-second stagger. These are one-shot entrances, not scroll-scrubbed opacity effects. Nested copy is animated only once, card containers no longer compete with their text, and story panes/diagrams retain their separate motion owner. Restored/in-view text is not hidden again; keyboard focus exposes an action immediately. Allocation assembly and the reading-progress line remain. Financial values are never interpolated or synthesized. Default content and CTAs are visible without JavaScript.

Reduced motion removes all pins, extended story spacing, parallax and entrances. Research views and the complete diagram remain available through manual tabs, direct steps and Next controls. Media changes and route navigation revert only this page's GSAP contexts; observers and listeners are cleaned up. Font, research and FAQ layout changes refresh trigger positions through coalesced observers.

Research tabs support arrow, Home and End keys. Mobile navigation supports Escape and restores focus to its trigger. Section links use Next-aware navigation so browser Back restores the landing route and its animation lifecycle after an in-page jump. FAQs use native details/summary behavior. Light/dark preferences and the existing privacy controls are preserved. There is no smooth-scroll replacement, mandatory scroll snapping or wheel/touch interception.

The final CTA now belongs to a single forest-green footer, alongside the brand, product and community links, risk disclosure and legal/data controls. Its reading text reveals as one group on approach so the final legal links cannot stay concealed near the maximum scroll position. The global privacy component previously added a second footer beneath the landing footer. It now omits only that footer on `/` and `/landing`, while continuing to own consent restoration, the essential-storage banner, optional analytics gating and storage-error notices. The landing's Privacy choices button reopens that same banner; other routes retain their data/security footer.
